Your brand is one of your most valuable assets — and attackers know it. By impersonating a trusted organisation, criminals can trick customers into handing over payment details, persuade employees to share passwords, or damage a reputation that took years to build.

## Common impersonation tactics

- **Look-alike domains.** A single changed letter or an extra word in a domain name, used for phishing emails and fake login pages.
- **Cloned websites.** Copies of real sites that collect credentials or payments.
- **Fake social media accounts.** Pages posing as a company, or as its executives, offering fake promotions, jobs or support.
- **Fraudulent apps and ads.** Imitation mobile apps and paid search ads that send users to criminal sites.
- **Leaked data for sale.** Stolen credentials and documents traded on the dark web, used to make scams more convincing.

## Why early detection matters

Impersonation campaigns move quickly. A fake site can be registered, promoted and used against customers within hours. The longer it stays online, the more people it reaches — and the more support calls, refunds and reputational harm follow.

## Practical steps to protect your brand

1. **Monitor continuously.** Watch for new domains, sites, social accounts and apps that use your name or logo. [Brand monitoring](/services/threat-intelligence-brand-protection) combines keyword, image and signature-based detection to find them fast.
2. **Act on what you find.** Work with domain registrars, hosting providers and social platforms to take fraudulent content down.
3. **Secure your own email domain.** Email authentication (SPF, DKIM and DMARC) makes it much harder for attackers to send email that appears to come from you.
4. **Watch the dark web.** Early warning of leaked credentials lets you reset passwords before they are abused.
5. **Tell customers how you communicate.** Publish which channels you use, and remind customers you will never ask for passwords or one-time codes.
6. **Train your people.** Staff are targeted too — [awareness training](/services/security-awareness-training) helps them recognise impersonation attempts.
